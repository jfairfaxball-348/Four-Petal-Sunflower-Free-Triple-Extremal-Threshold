# S002 — Independent 13-support lazy-MILP attack; OPEN outcome

Theorem (simple soundness and lossless anchor, not novel). For every nonempty simple 3-uniform family of 40 edges supported on at most 13 vertices, a relabelling into [0,12] sends some chosen edge to (0,1,2). A four-sunflower-free such family must satisfy each pair-codegree inequality d(x,y)<=3 and each exclusion inequality x_a+x_b+x_c+x_d<=3 for a quartet of edges (a,b,c,d) with six equal set intersections. Thus the lazy MILP is a *sound relaxation* at each iteration, and would validate a solution only after complete independent oracle checking. Proof: relabel the support injectively, permute the chosen triple to (0,1,2); pair codegree 4 gives pair-core sunflower; quartet cutting excludes precisely a forbidden quartet. Every actual 40-family satisfies these necessary constraints. No assertion about n>13 follows.

Run (frozen S002 v1, see experiments files) collected 12,095 explicit quartet cuts across 40 integer trial assignments. Each assignment was invalid; procedure stopped at 40-round cap without infeasibility. Hence original inequalities 39<=f(3,4)<=49 do not change.

Proof-first alternative from PRIOR ART, not claimed new: link graphs have maximum degree <=3 and matching number <=3. By Chvátal–Hanson CH(3,3)=10 (already in Axante et al. Lemmas 2.2/2.3), d_F(x)<=10 for each vertex. Therefore every 40-edge family has >=ceil(120/10)=12 support vertices, and any 12-vertex 40-edge example must be degree-ten regular. Use this equality case for a future lossless finite reduction, if any, but do not assert that 13 points suffice universally. General no-4-disjoint-edge condition ν(F)<=3 also from prior art. If ν(F)=3, union of three disjoint edges intersects every edge (otherwise ν>=4); no claim if ν<3 that a 9-vertex transversal was actually constructed.

Novelty: no qualifying new theorem for >=40 / <=48, no new global bound or priority clearance. The separation engineering and bounded negative partial evidence are search artefacts, not publishable mathematical results.
